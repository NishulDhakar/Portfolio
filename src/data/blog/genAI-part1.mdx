# GenAI Part 1: Understanding the Jargon

GenAI sounds intimidating because of the **terminology**, not because it's inherently complex.

Words like:

> transformer, embeddings, attention, softmax

make people think this is PhD-level material.

**It's not.** Let's break down each term with simple definitions and examples.

---

## The Big Picture Flow

Here's the complete flow of how GenAI works. Keep this mental model in mind as we explore each component:

```mermaid
Text
 ↓
Tokenization (vocab size decides how text is split)
 ↓
Tokens → IDs
 ↓
Embeddings (vectors) + Positional Encoding
 ↓
Transformer
 ├─ Encoder (understanding)
 │    └─ Self-Attention
 │        └─ Multi-Head Attention
 ↓
Contextual Vectors (Semantic Meaning)
 ↓
Decoder (generation)
 ↓
Linear Layer
 ↓
Softmax + Temperature (temp)
 ↓
Next Token Prediction
 ↓
Output Text
```

Everything below explains this flow **step by step**.

---

## Text

**Definition:** What humans write.

**Example:** `"I want to learn GenAI"`

AI doesn't understand this directly. This is raw input that needs processing.

---

## Tokenization

**Definition:** Breaking text into smaller pieces called **tokens**.

**Example:**

```
"I want to learn GenAI"
↓
["I", " want", " to", " learn", " Gen", "AI"]
```

**Why tokenize?**
- Easier for computers to process
- Handles new words better
- Saves memory

---

## Vocab Size

**Definition:** The total number of tokens the model knows.

**Example:**
- Vocab size = 50,000
- This means the model has 50,000 known token pieces

<Badge>Key Insight</Badge> Small vocab means more splitting. Large vocab means less splitting.

---

## Tokens to IDs

**Definition:** Each token gets replaced by a unique number.

**Example:**

```
"I" → 101
"Gen" → 5021
"AI" → 982
```

Why? Because **computers work with numbers, not words.**

---

## Vectors

**Definition:** A list of numbers representing something.

**Example:**

```
"cat" → [0.2, -1.3, 4.1, ...]
```

Think of vectors as **coordinates in meaning-space**.

---

## Embeddings

**Definition:** Vectors that capture **meaning**.

**Example:**
- `"dog"` and `"puppy"` have similar embeddings
- `"dog"` and `"laptop"` have very different embeddings

This is how AI understands:

> "These words are related."

---

## Positional Encoding

**Definition:** Information about **word order**.

**Example:**

```
"Dog bites man"
vs
"Man bites dog"
```

Same words. **Completely different meanings.**

Positional encoding tells the model:

> "This word came first, that came later."

---

## Transformer

**Definition:** The core architecture behind modern GenAI models.

Think of it as:

> A machine that understands relationships between words **simultaneously**, rather than sequentially.

**Everything important happens inside transformers.**

---

## Encoder

**Definition:** The part of the transformer that **understands input**.

**Used in models like:**
- BERT
- Search engines
- Classification tasks

The encoder focuses on:

> "What does this text mean?"

---

## Self-Attention

**Definition:** Each word examines other words and determines **what matters**.

**Example:**

```
"The student solved the problem because he practiced."
```

The word `"he"` refers to:
- `student` ✓
- `problem` ✗

**Self-attention figures out these relationships.**

---

## Multi-Head Attention

**Definition:** Multiple self-attention mechanisms running in parallel.

Each **head** focuses on different aspects:
- Grammar
- Meaning
- Long-distance context

It's like reading the same sentence through **multiple lenses simultaneously**.

---

## Contextual Vectors

**Definition:** Embeddings enriched with context after attention processing.

**Example:**

The word `"bank"` means:
- River bank in one sentence
- Financial institution in another

**Contextual vectors change based on usage.**

---

## Semantic Meaning

**Definition:** The actual understanding of meaning, not just words.

This isn't a single step but **emerges** after attention mechanisms and multiple layers process the information.

---

## Decoder

**Definition:** The part of the transformer that **generates text**.

**Used in models like:**
- GPT
- ChatGPT
- Text generation models

The decoder continuously asks:

> "What token should come next?"

---

## Linear Layer

**Definition:** Converts internal vectors into scores for each possible token.

Think of it as:

> "Prepare answer candidates for all possible next words."

---

## Softmax

**Definition:** Converts scores into **probabilities**.

**Example:**

```
Scores:
["AI": 0.6, "ML": 0.3, "pizza": 0.1]
```

Now the model can make a selection based on these probabilities.

---

## Temperature

**Definition:** Controls **randomness** in output.

**Example:**
- **Low temperature** → safe, predictable answers
- **High temperature** → creative, unpredictable answers

Same model. **Different behavior.**

---

## Next Token Prediction

**Definition:** The fundamental operation of GenAI.

The model does **one thing**: predict the next token.

It repeats this process continuously to generate complete text.

---

## Knowledge Cutoff

**Definition:** The date until which the model was trained.

**Example:**
- Model trained through 2024
- Doesn't know about 2025 events

This isn't a limitation by design, just a **constraint of the training data**.

---

## Final Summary

> **GenAI = text → tokens → numbers → attention → meaning → probabilities → next token**

That's it. No magic involved. Just sophisticated pattern matching at scale.
